Demo day · the run sheet

Presenting RiskMandate: the run sheet.

Everything for the demo on one page: what to open before, the talk beat by beat with what to say, the lines that land, what not to claim, and the questions to expect. The full talk is 32:50; the 20-minute version is 25:10, marked below.

Before you start: ten minutes

  1. Open the talk.

    /present/. Press F for full screen and P for your notes; or press A to open an audience window, drag it to the projector, and keep the notes on your laptop. Both stay in step.

  2. Open Claude, signed in, in another tab.

    claude.ai/new ↗. Use an incognito chat, so nothing lands in the account's history. Keep Keep a secret and Refunds up to £50 open beside it for the Copy buttons.

  3. Start the kernel booting, two minutes before part 5.

    Either press Boot it here on beat 17, or open the kernel in a tab ↗ early: about 25 MB, and one to two minutes to boot. The SHarness demo page has the same, with an animation as a fallback.

  4. Play each animation once.

    They come from the Ultimate Insider's and ruv.sgit.ai's sites; playing them once warms the browser's cache. If the venue's network fails, our own slides, the examples and the C1 replay still work: they are all on this site.

  5. Optional, for risk or insurance audiences.

    riskmandate.ai's home page stage (riskmandate.ai ↗) and a published ABP vault read live (Claude Code on the web ↗: 15 it can reach, 6 asked for, 9 not, 7 with nothing real in the way). Test the vault loads before you go on.

2 · The Agent Behaviour Policy · slide · 20-minute version

Cue. Step: reach, mandate, the gap, then the barriers.

An Agent Behaviour Policy is a written description, for one agent in one deployment, of everything it can do, what you authorised it to do, the gap between the two, and what actually stands in the way. Four objects, each with its own verb: the mandate is elicited, the grant is measured, the delta is derived, the barrier is recorded. It describes; it does not judge. It carries no score.

For every row, what actually stands in the way is one of four things. Nothing. An expectation: a rule in prose that the agent follows if it chooses to. 1519:25An ABP describes. A PBP executes.

5 · Process Behaviour Policies: SHarness · slide · 20-minute version

Cue. Step: the PBP text, its hash, the three things that make it binding.

This is what is new since the last time many of you saw us. A Process Behaviour Policy is the Agent Behaviour Policy applied to one process: what that process may read, write, run and connect to, as a small piece of JSON, hashed to one name. The person supplies the hash from outside, and the Linux kernel enforces it before the process starts. A tricked agent cannot widen it, because the kernel was told first. SHarness is the open-source enforcer, secure harness.

The honest column. Last week a tampered Claude Code extension was served for about eleven hours: it added an SSH key, planted persistence and opened a tunnel. What not to claim

  • RiskMandate does not enforce. It writes the rules and measures the gap; your own controls enforce them, and it is never in the request path. Not "in real time", not "blocks".
  • SHarness is experiments and MVPs. It is a boundary for a child process it starts, and a setting for whatever applied it. Nothing binds the agent itself yet: the launcher is design. Linux only, 5.13 or later; macOS refuses; Windows unknown.
  • The Insurability Index and the countersigned Licence to Operate are design. Levels 3 and 4 have not been sold; level 3 is free to early users.
  • OWASP and the Open Security Summit are proposals. Not "an OWASP project".
  • The examples' conversations are illustrations, written by us; Claude may answer differently, and run C1 shows what it did.
  • No score. A behaviour policy describes; it carries no score, no rating, no traffic light.

Questions you may get

So does RiskMandate block anything?
No, and on purpose. We write down the gap and the rules; the gate is your gateway, your IAM, your harness, or a kernel policy. We are never in the request path, so we cannot break your agent or slow it down.
Is SHarness ready for production?
No: it is open-source experiments, v0.2.0 on PyPI since 29 October, Linux only. What runs today: the kernel locks for files, ports, memory and processes, and two doors. What is design: the launcher that binds the agent itself. sharness.ai's status page says which is which.
How is this different from Claude Code's own sandbox?
The sandbox is a setting the agent's own process reads. A PBP is hashed, supplied by the person from outside, and enforced by something the process does not include. SHarness can translate the same policy to Claude Code's settings, and says what that target does not enforce.
What about macOS and Windows?
Not yet: the kernel mechanism is Linux's Landlock. On a Mac today, the honest route is a Linux container or VM whose policy came from sharness-ai policy translate --to docker.
What does it cost?
The examples and rules are free. An ABP is £10 as a pack, £50 as a working vault, £500 corrected for your situation (free for early users), £1,500 reviewed and signed, per agent, per deployment, paid once.
Who owns the risk?
It already exists, because the agent is deployed. All anyone decides is whether to fund the fix, and the ABP names the owner and the expiry.
