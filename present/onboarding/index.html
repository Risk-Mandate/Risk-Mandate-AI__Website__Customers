How it fits, from your side

From one line to a boundary: what you do, and what you get.

RiskMandate writes down what your agent can reach, what you authorised, the gap, and what actually stands in the way; your own controls enforce it, and we are never in the request path. Here is the whole of it, bottom to top, with what is live and what is not yet.

Your first agent, in steps

  1. S0 · In, with credit.

    £50 of credit for reading this site, and the six examples. Nothing to install.

  2. S1 · One agent named.

    One agent you actually run, in one deployment, matched to the nearest of sixteen published examples.

  3. S2 · Its grant, measured.

    What it can reach, from its deployment and credentials: a prompt you run, or the documentation where you cannot.

  4. S3 · Its mandate, in your words.

    What you want it to do, what you do not, and what nobody mentioned. The correction is the elicitation.

  5. S4 · The gap, and the first finding.

    The rows it can reach that nobody authorised, and the one that surprises you.

  6. S5 · Hooks and boundaries.

    The rows the harness can hold become hooks; the rows that need a boundary get one, in your gateway, your IAM, or a kernel policy.

  7. S6 · The usage moment.

    In your words: what it is worth, and what a fair price is.
