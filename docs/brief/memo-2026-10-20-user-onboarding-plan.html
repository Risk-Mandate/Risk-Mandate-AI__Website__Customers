What it adds to the brief

Read against the brief, the memo makes five things concrete. The transcript is the lead's spoken words; the list is the build session's reading of them, and is the part that can be wrong.

  1. The first users are named. The lead, the co-founder ("Nime" in the transcript is Nimay Parekh, the co-founder and chief executive, as on the about page), and the agents the lead already runs: about ten, probably more, which are the ones that need behaviour policies. The brief said "some of it is us"; the memo says it is us first.
  2. The programme is one people propose to join, because capacity is limited. The brief's mailing list for the next batch is the same mechanism seen from the other side.
  3. What the behaviour policies are. Very explicit ones that work with Claude and with ChatGPT, covering two of the three places a behaviour policy can run: the rows the agent follows, and the rows the hooks hold. The third place is left for later. This is the step S5 on the milestone ladder.
  4. The wired-in agent produces the end behaviour policy. The agent that is already wired in gets the responsibility and the requirement to create the end behaviour policy, which means the workflow has to be implemented with it and the KPIs and objectives mapped out. This is the proposed row programme.agent-policies in the reality file.
  5. The milestone. To get a behaviour policy, and to find behaviour policies that add value to these users; and to focus on the onboarding and the experience, as smooth and effective as it can be, to the point where it adds a lot of value and they understand what the behaviour policy is doing. The brief's "usage moment" and "entry point with least friction", in the lead's own words.
