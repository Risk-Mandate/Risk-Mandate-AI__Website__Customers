Nadia Farouk: Can I stop ChatGPT sending an email in my name before I have seen it?

Invented, not user research. Everybody in this study is invented, and their words were written by a model playing them. What they saw is real: each screenshot is what the browser showed at that step. They find what a page fails to say; they cannot tell us what a customer wants.

Product manager at a 200-person retailer. Uses ChatGPT with the Gmail connector, and Claude in the browser. Not a developer. From a post by the lead on LinkedIn, on her phone, between two meetings. Lands on the home page.

Knows
  • ChatGPT, custom instructions, connectors in settings
  • what a prompt is
Does not know
  • what a hook is
  • what CLAUDE.md or a terminal is
  • what Claude Code is
  • what an ABP is
Patience
four minutes on a phone
Leaves if
  • the first screen is for developers
  • she meets three words she does not know in a row
  • it asks her to install something
We expected
Nadia is put off by 'hook' and 'Claude Code' in the hero, scrolls, and may find the ChatGPT section or the send-as-you policy.
Ended
got-it: She found the page that gives her a line to paste into ChatGPT's custom instructions so nothing is sent in her name without a yes, though the page itself says the line is only an expectation, not a guarantee.

The interview

In one sentence, what is this site?
A shop of short pasteable rules and small settings that tell an AI assistant what it must not do without asking, where you pay a few pence to read each page.
Was any of it for you? Which part?
Yes, one page: 'Nothing is sent in your name until you have seen it'. It is my question almost word for word, for ChatGPT custom instructions. The home page and the first card on the list were not for me.
Did you believe it? What made you believe it, or not?
Mostly. The before-and-after test prompt and the mock-up made it feel real, and it was honest that the line is only an expectation and not a lock. That honesty made me trust it more, but it also left me unsure it will really hold with the Gmail connector.
Where did you have to guess?
What the 50 pounds was and why it dropped as I scrolled. What a 'hook' is. Whether 'follows it when it can' means it sometimes sends anyway. And what 'Half' meant in the table.
What did you still not know at the end?
Whether ChatGPT with the Gmail connector really obeys the line, whether there is a proper lock for ChatGPT that I can set myself, and whether the 50 pounds is real money I could be charged.
Was the credit and the price of each page right for what you got?
50p for the page I needed felt fair, and it said so at the top. But the home page and first list card did not say the price of reading, and I paid in small amounts as I scrolled without being told on that screen. I would have paid the 50p for a firmer answer.
Would you install a behaviour policy from here, today? If not, what would have to change?
I would paste the line into custom instructions tonight, since it is just text and not an install. I would not install anything that needs a terminal or a hook. To go further I would need the page to say plainly whether it holds for ChatGPT with Gmail, and a way to check it myself.
One change. What is it?
Put my question first on the home page: 'Stop ChatGPT sending email as you', in plain words, instead of a headline about hooks and Claude Code.
Would you send this to somebody else? Who?
Yes, to the other product manager on my team who also lets ChatGPT draft her emails, but I would send her the link to the email page, not the home page.
Rate the experience from 1 to 5, and say why in one sentence.
3: the email page gave me what I came for and was honest about its limits, but the first screen was not for me and it took four taps and a lot of skipped jargon to reach it.
