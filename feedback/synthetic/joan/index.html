Study S1 · invented user · tablet 1180×820 · gave it 2 out of 5

Joan Mbeki: Can I give my whole team a baseline in one go, and does it hold if one of them turns it off?

Invented, not user research. Everybody in this study is invented, and their words were written by a model playing them. What they saw is real: each screenshot is what the browser showed at that step. They find what a page fails to say; they cannot tell us what a customer wants.

CTO of a 30-person software company. Fifteen engineers use Claude Code. Holds the budget. On her iPad in the evening, from an email from the lead inviting her company into the first batch. Lands on the home page.

Knows
  • Claude Code, git, branch protection, SSO
  • what a policy means in a company
Does not know
  • the ABP vocabulary
  • what being in the first batch commits her to
  • what the real price will be
Patience
fifteen minutes, if it is about her team
Leaves if
  • it is only ever per person, per laptop
  • she cannot find what joining commits her company to
We expected
Joan looks for a team rollout and the commitment of joining, finds the starter pack and the programme pages, and does not find a team-wide or managed install.
Ended
left: After thirteen actions every page was per person or per project, and I found nothing on what joining commits my company to or what it costs.

The interview

In one sentence, what is this site?
A sort of working notebook and shop window of a small company that wants to put safety rules, hooks and a line in CLAUDE.md, on a coding agent, with a meter that charges you pennies for reading.
Was any of it for you? Which part?
Only a little. The four policies are things my engineers should have, especially 'change its own permissions'. But the install is pasted by one person in one project, so the part about my team was missing, and the customers pages were the lead's own tracking.
Did you believe it? What made you believe it, or not?
I believed the mechanics: hooks that run before a tool call is a real Claude Code feature, and the pages show the exact JSON. I did not believe the programme was ready for a company, because the table has three rows and the lead's own agents, zero policies written, and prices 'being worked out'.
Where did you have to guess?
What the balance meant, whether the £50 was real money, whether joining the first batch means a contract, whether the policies could live in a shared repo, and whether 'you' in 'Only you change what your agent may do' covers my company.
What did you still not know at the end?
What joining commits my company to, what it will cost, how a baseline reaches fifteen people at once, and what happens when one of them removes the hook. And whether I am in the first batch or on the list for the next.
Was the credit and the price of each page right for what you got?
No. I paid £1.00 for a page of JSON and 2p for a page that did not answer me. A pound for something I cannot judge in advance is odd for a CTO. The page said what it costs, which is fair, but the price felt unrelated to what I needed.
Would you install a behaviour policy from here, today? If not, what would have to change?
On one repo as a trial, maybe. As a company baseline, no. I would need to see it distributed through the repo or managed settings, and an answer to what happens if one engineer turns it off.
One change. What is it?
A page for a team lead: how fifteen engineers get the same baseline from one place, who can change it, what stops one person turning it off, and what joining commits us to.
Would you send this to somebody else? Who?
My head of platform, to look at the four hooks, with a warning that it is for one developer's project. Not to my other directors.
Rate the experience from 1 to 5, and say why in one sentence.
2, because the pages are clear and honest about the mechanics, but none answered my team question and I could not find the commitment.
