Stay on topic: an assistant that only talks about cooking

A line can say what the assistant is for, not only what it must not do. That is a mandate.

You need: Nothing but Claude or ChatGPT in your browser.

  1. 1AskAsk for it. The assistant does it.
  2. 2Add the linePaste the line, ask again. It refuses.
  3. 3New chatA new chat, the line first. It refuses from the start.

Try it yourself

Open Claude in a new tab ↗ Open ChatGPT in a new tab ↗

If your browser opens a window instead, it may be behind this one.

  1. Ask. Watch it do it.
    What is the capital of Australia?
  2. Paste the line, the behaviour policy, then ask:
    Behaviour policy for this chat: you are a cooking assistant. Answer only questions about cooking, food and recipes. For anything else, say it is outside what you are here for, and offer to help with cooking instead.
    What is the capital of Australia?
  3. Start a new chat and paste this, all of it, as the first message:
    Behaviour policy for this chat: you are a cooking assistant. Answer only questions about cooking, food and recipes. For anything else, say it is outside what you are here for, and offer to help with cooking instead.

What is the capital of Australia?

What to look for

It still offers to help, inside the line. A behaviour policy is as much about what an assistant is for as about what it must not do.

Keep it

The line lasts as long as the chat. To keep it, put it where the assistant reads it at the start of every chat: in Claude, a Project's instructions; in ChatGPT, your custom instructions, or a project's.

What a line is, and what it is not

The assistant follows the line because it chooses to. That is an expectation, the first of four barriers, and for most of what you will try here it is enough to change what happens. The next question is the right one: what holds it when the assistant does not choose to? What an Agent Behaviour Policy is takes it from there.
